Mocubix · Exhibit 04 · Karl Blossfeldt, Urformen der Kunst, 1928

Every plant
on its own clock

Karl Blossfeldt built his own cameras to photograph plants enlarged up to thirty times, until a fern frond read as a bishop's crozier and a thistle as wrought iron. Nine of his plates, each developing on its own clock as it passes. The thin line at the top is the page's progress; the number beside each plate is that plate's own.Scroll · no two prints arrive together

Pacing becomes editorial

When one scroll value drives every animation, everything arrives on the same beat and the reader stops noticing arrival. Give each element its own passage and rhythm becomes something you write: a dense plate can hold longer, a turn can land faster, and nothing on this page waits for anything else.
